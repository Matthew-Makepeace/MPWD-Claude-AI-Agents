What is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that your dentist places directly into the jawbone, where it acts as an artificial tooth root. Over a few months, the bone grows around it in a process called osseointegration — the implant essentially becomes part of your jaw. Once it's fully integrated, a custom-made crown is attached on top, giving you a tooth that looks, feels, and functions like the real thing. What makes implants the gold standard for replacing a missing tooth is that they work from the root up. Unlike bridges or dentures, an implant preserves your jawbone, keeps neighbouring teeth from shifting, and doesn't rely on anything else in your mouth for support. Done well, an implant can last a lifetime. With over 30 years of implant experience, Dr. Sezer has placed countless implants for patients across Toronto — and his approach has always been the same: get it right the first time, so you don't have to think about it again.

When Should You Get One?

  • You've lost a single tooth. An implant fills the gap without touching the healthy teeth on either side.
  • You're missing multiple teeth. Implants can support individual crowns or serve as anchors for a bridge — no need to replace every tooth separately.
  • You have a tooth that can't be saved. Removing it and placing an implant protects the surrounding bone and closes the space cleanly.
  • Your dentures don't feel secure. Implant-supported dentures snap firmly into place, eliminating slipping, clicking, and dietary restrictions.
  • You've been told you're losing bone in your jaw. An implant stops that process and keeps your facial structure intact.

What to Expect

  1. Your Consultation. Dr. Sezer reviews your history, takes imaging of your jaw, and walks you through your treatment — no surprises, no pressure.
  2. Implant Placement. The titanium post is placed under local anaesthetic. Most patients find it far more comfortable than expected.
  3. Your New Tooth. Once the implant integrates with the bone, your custom crown is fitted. You leave with a tooth that matches your smile and holds up to everyday life.

What are Porcelain Veneers?

Porcelain veneers are ultra-thin ceramic shells — roughly the thickness of a contact lens — bonded permanently to the front surface of your teeth. They're one of the most versatile cosmetic treatments in dentistry, able to address a wide range of concerns in a relatively small number of appointments.

Before the veneers go on, a small amount of enamel is gently removed from the tooth surface to ensure a natural fit and thickness. Impressions or digital scans are taken and sent to our in-house lab, where your veneers are custom-fabricated in porcelain that reflects light the way real enamel does — so they look genuinely alive, not artificial.

The finish is durable, stain-resistant, and built to last. Most patients get eight to ten veneers across their upper front teeth, though the number depends entirely on your goals. The result is a consistent, refined smile that still looks like yours.

When Should You Get One?

  • Your teeth are chipped, cracked, or worn down from grinding.
  • You have stubborn staining that whitening cannot fully correct.
  • Your teeth are uneven in length, shape, or size.
  • You have small gaps you'd like to close without braces.
  • You want a long-lasting smile upgrade, not a quick fix.

What to Expect

  1. Smile Design Consultation. Dr. Sezer reviews your goals, takes photos and measurements, and walks you through what your finished smile will look like before any work begins.
  2. Tooth Preparation and Temporaries. A thin layer of enamel is removed, impressions or scans are taken, and temporary veneers are placed while your custom set is made in-house.
  3. Bonding and Final Fitting. Your porcelain veneers are bonded to your teeth, shaped, and polished until the fit and look are exactly right.

What is Teeth Whitening?

Zoom! is a professional in-office whitening system that uses a specially formulated hydrogen peroxide gel activated by a light source to break apart stains on and within your enamel. The whole process takes about an hour — you sit back, the light does the work, and you leave noticeably brighter.

What sets professional whitening apart from over-the-counter products isn't just the strength of the formula — it's the control. Dr. Sezer's team monitors the process throughout, adjusting as needed to keep your gums protected and your results even. There's no risk of patches, no guessing on trays, and no waiting weeks to see if something worked.

Most patients see their teeth lighten by several shades in one session. Results vary depending on the type and depth of staining, but the outcome is consistently better than anything you'll get from a strip or a box. And it lasts — especially with basic maintenance.

When Should You Get One?

  • Your teeth have yellowed from coffee, tea, wine, or tobacco.
  • You have a special event — wedding, interview, or reunion — coming up.
  • You've tried whitening strips and weren't impressed with the results.
  • Your smile feels dull despite regular brushing and professional cleanings.
  • You want a quick, visible improvement without any dental work.

What to Expect

  1. Pre-Whitening Check. Before the session, your teeth and gums are assessed to confirm whitening is a good fit and your starting shade is recorded for comparison.
  2. The Zoom! Session. A protective covering is placed over your gums, the whitening gel is applied, and the Zoom! light activates the formula — typically in three 15-minute rounds.
  3. Immediate Results. You leave the same day with a visibly brighter smile, along with take-home tips to maintain your results and manage any sensitivity.

What is Invisalign?

Invisalign is an orthodontic system that uses a series of custom-made, clear plastic aligners to gradually shift your teeth into the correct position. Each set of aligners is worn for about two weeks before you move to the next — no adjustments, no tightening, no metal in your mouth.

The process begins with a 3D scan of your teeth, which creates a digital model of how your bite looks today and maps the full movement to where it needs to go. You can see your projected outcome before treatment even starts.

Because the aligners are removable, you eat whatever you want and clean your teeth normally. Appointments are shorter and less frequent than with traditional braces, and most people around you won't even notice you're wearing them. Treatment time varies but typically runs between 12 and 18 months, depending on the complexity of your case.

When Should You Get One?

  • Your teeth are crowded, crooked, or have shifted over time.
  • You have gaps between your teeth you'd like to close.
  • You want to straighten your teeth without visible metal braces.
  • Your bite is slightly off and causing uneven wear on your teeth.
  • You had braces as a teen but your teeth have moved since.

What to Expect

  1. 3D Scan and Treatment Preview. Dr. Sezer scans your teeth digitally and shows you a simulation of your full tooth movement — from where you are now to where you want to be — before you commit to anything.
  2. Wearing Your Aligners. You receive your first sets of aligners and wear each one for roughly two weeks, checking in with the clinic periodically to ensure everything is tracking as planned.
  3. Finishing and Retention. Once your teeth have reached their final position, a custom retainer keeps everything in place so your results stay exactly where they should.

What is Root Canal Treatment?

A root canal treats infection inside your tooth — specifically in the soft pulp tissue and canals that run through the root. When bacteria get into this area (usually through a crack, cavity, or injury), the infection can become painful and, if left untreated, spread to the surrounding bone.

During the procedure, Dr. Sezer gently removes the infected pulp, cleans and shapes the canal space, and seals it to prevent re-infection. The area is fully numbed beforehand, so most patients are surprised by how comfortable the process actually is. It typically takes one to two appointments.

Once the root canal is complete, a crown is usually placed over the tooth to protect it and restore full function. With proper care, a treated tooth can last a lifetime — keeping your natural smile intact instead of moving to extraction and replacement.

When Should You Get One?

  • You have a persistent toothache that doesn't ease on its own.
  • A tooth is sensitive to hot or cold long after the source is removed.
  • You notice swelling, tenderness, or a pimple-like bump on your gum.
  • A tooth has been cracked or injured and is causing ongoing pain.
  • Your dentist has found deep decay that has reached the nerve.

What to Expect

  1. Consultation and Diagnosis. Dr. Sezer reviews your symptoms and uses imaging to assess the extent of infection before recommending treatment.
  2. The Procedure. With the area comfortably numbed, Dr. Sezer removes the infected tissue, cleans the canals, and seals the tooth — usually completed in a single visit.
  3. Recovery and Restoration. Mild soreness for a day or two is normal; once a crown is placed, your tooth is fully restored and ready for everyday use.

What is a Composite Filling?

A filling restores a tooth that has been damaged by decay, returning it to its original shape and function. Composite (tooth-coloured) fillings are made from a mixture of glass and resin, and they bond directly to your tooth — which means less removal of healthy tooth structure than traditional amalgam fillings typically require.

The process is straightforward. Dr. Sezer removes the decayed portion of the tooth, prepares the surface, and applies the composite material in thin layers. Each layer is hardened with a curing light, then shaped and polished to match the natural contours of your tooth. Most fillings are completed in a single appointment.

Beyond aesthetics, composite fillings are a strong, long-lasting choice. They support the remaining tooth structure and hold up well under everyday chewing. And if you have old silver amalgam fillings you'd like replaced for cosmetic or health reasons, that's a conversation we're happy to have at your visit.

When Should You Get One?

  • A dentist has identified a cavity that needs to be treated.
  • You have tooth sensitivity to sweets, heat, or cold.
  • You can see a dark spot or visible hole forming on a tooth.
  • An existing silver filling is cracked, worn, or you'd prefer a natural look.
  • You've chipped a tooth and want a seamless, colour-matched repair.

What to Expect

  1. Examination and Diagnosis. Dr. Sezer checks for decay using visual examination and X-rays to confirm the extent of damage before proceeding.
  2. The Filling Appointment. The area is numbed, the decay removed, and the composite resin is applied, shaped, and polished to match your tooth — typically in under an hour.
  3. Same-Day Results. You leave with a fully restored tooth that looks and feels natural, with no special aftercare beyond your regular brushing and flossing.

What is a Porcelain Crown?

A porcelain crown is a custom-made cap that fits over an existing tooth, restoring its shape, size, and strength. Crowns are used when a tooth is too damaged or decayed to be repaired with a filling alone — think of it as a protective shell that holds everything together while returning the tooth to full function.

Porcelain is the material of choice for visible teeth because it closely mimics the light-reflecting properties of natural enamel. Dr. Sezer takes precise impressions and 3D scans to ensure your crown fits perfectly and matches the colour of your surrounding teeth. Because we have an in-house lab, the dentist and technician work in close communication throughout fabrication — a level of attention most clinics simply can't offer.

The result is a tooth that looks completely natural, feels comfortable, and functions normally. Most patients can't tell the difference between their crown and the real thing — and that's exactly the point.

When Should You Get One?

  • A tooth is cracked, broken, or decayed beyond what a filling can fix.
  • Significant decay has weakened a tooth's structural integrity.
  • You need to protect a tooth following a root canal treatment.
  • A tooth is misshapen or severely discoloured and affects your confidence.
  • An existing crown is worn, cracked, or no longer fits properly.

What to Expect

  1. Consultation and Preparation. Dr. Sezer assesses the tooth, takes impressions or 3D scans, and prepares it to receive the crown — a temporary is placed while yours is fabricated in our lab.
  2. In-House Fabrication. Your crown is crafted on-site, with the fit, colour, and contour refined to match your natural teeth precisely and efficiently.
  3. Placement and Final Fit. At your second appointment, Dr. Sezer seats the permanent crown, adjusts your bite, and cements it into place for a result that looks and feels right.

What are Dentures?

Dentures are removable prosthetic teeth designed to replace missing teeth and the surrounding gum tissue. They come in two main types: complete dentures, which replace all teeth on either the upper or lower arch (or both), and partial dentures, which fill in the gaps when some natural teeth remain.

Modern dentures are far more comfortable and natural-looking than what previous generations experienced. At Dr. Sezer & Associates, dentures are custom-crafted in our in-house lab using precise measurements and careful colour matching, so they complement your facial structure and sit the way they should — securely and naturally.

Beyond appearance, dentures restore the practical essentials: the ability to chew a full range of foods, speak clearly, and feel confident in everyday life. For patients who want added stability, implant-supported dentures are also available — they anchor to dental implants and eliminate the need for adhesives entirely. Dr. Sezer will help you find the right option for your situation.

When Should You Get One?

  • You are missing several or all teeth on one or both arches.
  • Tooth loss is affecting your ability to chew or speak normally.
  • You feel self-conscious about visible gaps when you smile or talk.
  • An existing denture no longer fits well or is visibly worn.
  • You want a non-surgical option for full or significant tooth replacement.

What to Expect

  1. Assessment and Impressions. Dr. Sezer evaluates your mouth, takes detailed impressions, and walks you through your options — including full, partial, or implant-supported dentures.
  2. Custom Fabrication. Your dentures are crafted in our in-house lab with careful attention to fit, bite alignment, and the colour and shape of your replacement teeth.
  3. Fitting and Adjustments. Once your dentures are ready, Dr. Sezer fits them, confirms your comfort and bite, and schedules any follow-up adjustments until they feel exactly right.

What is Dental Bonding?

Dental bonding uses a composite resin — a durable, tooth-coloured material — that's applied directly to the tooth surface, sculpted carefully to shape, and hardened with a special curing light. The result is a repair so smooth it blends seamlessly with the rest of your natural smile.

The procedure is minimally invasive. In most cases, little to no enamel removal is required, which makes bonding one of the most conservative cosmetic options available. It's also one of the quickest — most treatments are completed in a single appointment, with no waiting on lab work and no temporaries to manage.

Dr. Sezer's team approaches every bonding case with an eye for detail built over 30+ years in aesthetic dentistry. With an in-house dental lab for precise colour and material matching, the final result doesn't just restore your tooth — it looks like the problem was never there.

When Should You Get One?

  • You have a chipped or cracked tooth that bothers you when you smile.
  • A small gap between your front teeth has always been a source of self-consciousness.
  • One tooth is noticeably shorter or misshapen compared to those around it.
  • A tooth has minor staining or discolouration that whitening hasn't resolved.
  • You want a quick cosmetic fix without crowns, veneers, or lab wait times.

What to Expect

  1. Consultation. Dr. Sezer evaluates your tooth, selects a resin shade that matches your natural teeth, and walks you through exactly what the result will look like.
  2. Bonding Appointment. The resin is applied, sculpted, and polished in-chair — most cases take under an hour and require no anaesthetic.
  3. Your Result. You leave with a fully restored tooth the same day, ready to smile without thinking twice.

What is Emergency Dentistry?

Emergency dentistry covers urgent situations that need immediate professional attention — things that can't and shouldn't wait for a scheduled appointment. This includes severe toothaches, chipped or fractured teeth, knocked-out teeth, lost fillings or crowns, dental abscesses, and injuries to the soft tissues of the mouth.

The goal of emergency care is twofold: relieve pain fast, then stabilize or treat the underlying problem. Depending on what's happened, that might mean a same-day repair, a temporary restoration, pain management, or — in the case of a knocked-out tooth — reimplantation, which is time-sensitive and must happen quickly for the best outcome.

Dr. Sezer's team has the training, the technology, and the calm clinical experience to handle dental emergencies with real precision. With in-house 3D imaging for fast, accurate diagnosis and 30+ years of complex case experience across Toronto and Hamilton, you're in steady hands exactly when you need it most.

When Should You Get One?

  • You have a severe, persistent toothache that is getting worse, not better.
  • A tooth has been knocked out or is significantly cracked or broken.
  • You've lost a crown or filling and the tooth is sensitive or exposed.
  • Your jaw, cheek, or gum is swollen and possibly infected.
  • You've had a mouth injury and aren't sure how serious it is.

What to Expect

  1. Same-Day Contact. Call either location, let the team know it's an emergency, and they'll fit you in as soon as possible while advising you on what to do in the meantime.
  2. Immediate Assessment. Dr. Sezer evaluates the situation using examination and, where needed, in-house 3D imaging to identify exactly what's happening and why.
  3. Relief and Next Steps. Pain is addressed first, then you'll receive either an immediate repair or a clear treatment plan so you leave knowing exactly what comes next.

What is a Dental Bridge?

A dental bridge is a fixed prosthetic that replaces one or more missing teeth. It uses the natural teeth on either side of the gap — called abutment teeth — as anchors. Those teeth are shaped to support crowns, and a false tooth (called a pontic) is suspended between them to fill the space.

The bridge is custom-made to match the shape, size, and colour of your natural teeth. Once cemented in place, it functions just like a real tooth — you can eat, speak, and smile without any movement or extended adjustment period.

Because Dr. Sezer's team fabricates restorations in their own in-house dental lab, the fit and aesthetics are controlled at every step. There's no outsourcing, no guesswork, and less waiting. Your bridge is crafted with precision, checked against your bite, and placed only when it meets the standard this clinic is known for.

When Should You Get One?

  • You are missing one or more teeth and want a fixed, non-removable solution.
  • You're not a candidate for implants due to bone loss or other health factors.
  • Adjacent teeth are healthy and strong enough to serve as anchor points.
  • A gap is causing neighbouring teeth to shift or your bite alignment to change.
  • You want to restore full chewing function and a natural-looking, complete smile.

What to Expect

  1. Consultation and Planning. Dr. Sezer assesses the gap, examines the anchor teeth, and discusses whether a bridge or an implant is the better long-term fit for your situation.
  2. Preparation and Impressions. The abutment teeth are carefully shaped, detailed impressions are taken, and the in-house lab begins fabricating a custom bridge matched precisely to your natural teeth.
  3. Fitting and Placement. Your bridge is checked for fit and bite alignment, adjusted as needed, and permanently cemented so you leave with a fully restored smile.

What is Pediatric Dentistry?

Pediatric dentistry focuses on the oral health of children from their very first teeth through their teenage years. It covers everything from routine checkups and cleanings to cavity treatment, fluoride applications, sealants, and early orthodontic monitoring.

But it's as much about communication as it is about clinical care. Children process dental visits differently than adults. A good pediatric approach means explaining what's happening in plain language, moving at a pace the child is comfortable with, and building trust so the visit doesn't become something they dread.

At Dr. Sezer & Associates, young patients are treated as individuals — not just small adults. The team takes time to help children feel oriented and at ease before any treatment begins. Parents are kept fully informed at every step, so no one leaves with unanswered questions. A positive early experience is the best investment in a lifetime of healthy dental habits.

When Should You Get One?

  • Your child is around age one, or their first tooth has just appeared.
  • Your child is due for a routine checkup, cleaning, or fluoride treatment.
  • You've noticed a cavity, tooth pain, or unusual discolouration in a baby tooth.
  • Your child has dental anxiety and needs a calm, unhurried approach.
  • You want an early assessment of how teeth and jaw development are progressing.

What to Expect

  1. A Gentle Introduction. The first visit is about getting comfortable — Dr. Sezer's team introduces your child to the environment and builds rapport before any examination begins.
  2. The Checkup. Teeth, gums, and bite development are assessed, and parents receive a clear, jargon-free summary of their child's oral health and any concerns found.
  3. A Plan That Grows with Them. Whether treatment is needed now or everything looks great, you leave with clear guidance on what to watch for and when to come back next.
